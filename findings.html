What the current website is costing you, and what the rebuild fixes

This page documents an independent audit of thecapelligroup.com and explains, in plain language, why the concept rebuild looks the way it does. Every number below was measured directly against the live site. Nothing is estimated.

Measured 2026-09-09 with an automated Chromium browser (Playwright) at phone and desktop sizes. Rebuild preview: index.html.

Summary

A 2013 page builder, on a 2026 phone

The current site was built with GoDaddy Website Builder 7.0, a product from around 2013. It works the way websites worked before most visitors carried a phone. Today, a person under a court order looking for your clinic on a phone gets a page shrunk to about 40 percent of its intended size, with no phone number they can tap.

Priority

The two findings that matter most

If nothing else on this page gets fixed, these two should. One is a safety issue. The other decides whether anyone on a phone can use the site at all.

Safety content

The published crisis line is the retired number

What we found
The psychotherapy page lists 1-800-273-8255 (TALK) as the crisis line. That number belonged to the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline, which transitioned to the three-digit 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in July 2022. The old number still forwards today, so nobody calling it is stranded. But it is no longer the published national standard, and a caller who half-remembers it may misdial.
Why it matters
This is a clinic whose work is domestic violence, trauma, and veterans. Some of the people reading that page are in genuine distress. On this site, above every other content issue, the crisis information has to be current and unmissable. This is a correction, not a criticism: the number was right when the page was written.
The fix
The rebuild uses 988 as the primary crisis number, adds the Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741), and adds the Veterans Crisis Line (988, then press 1), which fits the clinic's Fort Bliss and Military Veteran Safe Zone positioning. All three appear in a strip at the very top of the page, in a full crisis section, in the Spanish summary, in the footer, and in a persistent phone action bar. No local crisis number was added, because none was verified; national numbers are stable and safe to publish.
Mobile

No mobile viewport tag, so phones render the desktop page at 40 percent

What we found
The HTML contains zero occurrences of <meta name="viewport">. Without it, mobile Safari falls back to a 980-pixel virtual screen and shrinks the whole page to fit. On a 390-pixel iPhone the page reports its own width as 980px. Text renders at roughly 40 percent of its intended size. The screenshot at 390px shows the navigation cut off mid-word on the left and the logo running off the right edge.
Why it matters
The people most likely to look you up on a phone are the ones under a court order, often anxious, often Spanish-speaking, sometimes with a probation officer standing next to them. They get a page they have to pinch and squint at, with nothing they can tap.
The fix
The rebuild is designed phone-first and carries the viewport tag. It was verified at 375px and 1440px with zero horizontal scrolling. The phone number is a large tappable link in the hero, in the header, in every section that asks the visitor to call, and in a fixed action bar at the bottom of every phone screen alongside 988.

All findings

Defect, consequence, fix

Each item states what was measured, what it means for a visitor or for search, and what the rebuild does about it.

Contact

The phone number is not on the homepage, and nothing is tappable

What we found
Zero tel: links and zero mailto: links on the homepage. The number 915-799-0614 does not appear on the homepage at all. It lives on the contact page, as plain text.
Why it matters
Registration for every program starts with a phone call. A visitor on a phone has to find the contact page, then copy or memorize the number, then switch to the dialer. Each step loses people.
The fix
The rebuild has 22 tappable phone links and 4 tappable email links across one page, all pointing at the clinic's published number and address. The number is the largest text on the screen after the headline.
Speed

48 seconds to finish loading

What we found
Time to network idle was 48,013 milliseconds, measured identically on the mobile and desktop profiles.
Why it matters
Visitors do not wait 48 seconds. Google measures load time and ranks slow pages lower, especially on mobile.
The fix
The rebuild is a single HTML file with its styling inline. Its only outside request is two Google Fonts. There are no image files, no page-builder scripts, and no tracking beacons.
Search & accessibility

14 images, none with alt text

What we found
Every one of the 14 images on the homepage has no alt attribute.
Why it matters
A screen reader announces nothing useful for any of them. Google Images cannot index them. If the images carry the accreditation logos or the phone number, that information is invisible to both.
The fix
The rebuild uses no raster images at all. Its visuals are drawn in code and marked decorative, so assistive technology skips them. Every placeholder for a future photo is labeled as a placeholder and carries a text description. When real photographs are added, each one gets a written alt text.
Search & accessibility

14 top-level headings and no hierarchy

What we found
14 <h1> elements, 0 <h2>, 0 <h3>. Every headline on the page claims to be the page's title.
Why it matters
Screen-reader users navigate by heading level, so they cannot move through this page by structure. Google uses the single main heading to understand what a page is about, and here it gets fourteen competing answers.
The fix
One <h1>, twelve <h2> section headings, and <h3> beneath them, verified by rendering. The page also has a skip link and proper header, navigation, main, and footer landmarks.
Search

Google is left to guess what and where the business is

What we found
Zero structured data. The count of script[type="application/ld+json"] is 0. There is no machine-readable business type, address, hours, or location. There is also no canonical link tag, and the Open Graph URL points at an insecure http:// address ending in home-1.html rather than the site's root.
Why it matters
Structured data is how a clinic tells Google, in one block, "I am a medical business at this address with these hours." Without it, the knowledge panel, map listing, and hours display depend on Google inferring them from prose. The mismatched Open Graph URL means shares on social media point at the wrong address.
The fix
The rebuild includes a MedicalBusiness structured-data block with the clinic's published name, address, phone, email, service area, founders, opening hours, and languages. Nothing in it goes beyond what the clinic already publishes.
Content

Unedited page-builder placeholder text is live on the homepage

What we found
Rendered over the logo, visible to every visitor:
"You can add HTML directly into this element to render on the page. Just edit this element to add your own HTML."
Why it matters
It is the first sentence many visitors read. For a referral source deciding whether to trust a provider, it signals an unattended site.
The fix
Removed. The rebuild contains no builder-generated content.
Content

The page title, and therefore the Google result, has a typo

What we found
The homepage <title> reads "Anger Managment", missing the "e". That exact string is what appears in the browser tab and as the blue headline in Google search results.
Why it matters
It is the single most-seen piece of text on the site, and a misspelling in it costs credibility before the visitor arrives.
The fix
Corrected. The rebuild's title names the clinic, the three services, and the city.
Navigation

Ten navigation items, no hierarchy, two pointing at the wrong pages

What we found
The top navigation has 10 items in a flat row. Two labels do not match their destinations: "Battering Intervention & Prevention Program (BIPP)" points at a file named -alternative-dispute-resolution--adr-.html, and "Complementary and Integrative Health (CIH)" points at victim-impact-panel--vip-.html. The web addresses describe different services than the labels.
Why it matters
A probation officer looking for the BIPP page lands on a URL that says "alternative dispute resolution." Search engines read those URLs too, and index the wrong subject for the page.
The fix
The rebuild opens with three doors, one for each kind of visitor: court-ordered, seeking counseling, and referral source. The navigation has eight clearly named anchors on one page, including a marked Spanish entry, and every link lands where its label says.
Platform

Built on a page builder from around 2013

What we found
The generator tag reads Starfield Technologies; Go Daddy Website Builder 7.0.5350.
Why it matters
That product line was replaced years ago. It predates mobile-first design, does not emit modern accessibility markup, and is the root cause of most of the findings above. Fixing them one at a time inside it is not practical.
The fix
A rebuild rather than a patch. The concept is a single self-contained file with no builder, no plugins, and nothing to update or break.

What was built

One page that serves both kinds of visitor

Two audiences arrive at this site with different questions. The rebuild answers both without making either one hunt.

  • A hero that answers who, what, where, and "is it court-approved" on one screen, with the phone number as the largest tappable element and the BIPP provider number in view.
  • Crisis resources at the top, in a dedicated section, in Spanish, in the footer, and in the phone action bar. 988, Crisis Text Line, and the Veterans Crisis Line. Persistent without being alarming.
  • A "which describes you" router: court ordered, seeking counseling, or referral source. Each leads to the section written for them.
  • The 4-step BIPP intake as a visual sequence: Register, Evaluation, Orientation, Ongoing Groups. On the current site this is buried in a wall of text.
  • A referral-source section that puts Provider # 071-021, TDCJ-CJAD accreditation, the referral list, monthly progress reporting, and Fort Bliss coverage where a probation officer or judge will find them in ten seconds.
  • A real schedule for the four BIPP groups and the Saturday anger management group, with English and Spanish marked on each row. It stacks into cards on a phone.
  • Fees and insurance stated plainly: the $180 session rate, the discount categories, thirteen accepted plans, and payment methods. BIPP and anger management fees are described as set at registration, because the clinic does not publish them.
  • A Spanish summary block marked up as Spanish for screen readers and search engines, covering who the clinic is, what it does, the Tuesday Spanish group, the phone number, hours, and the 988 line.
  • Testimonials presented with restraint. Seven of the clinic's published quotes, verbatim, in a quiet grid. No carousel.
  • Every fact traceable. Nothing on the page was invented. Address, phone, hours, credentials, license numbers, schedules, prices, insurance, and quotes all come from the clinic's own published pages. Photos and the map are labeled placeholders.

Next steps

What it would take to go live

The concept is complete as a design and content proof. Four things are needed from the clinic before it could replace the current site.

  1. Confirm the facts. Phone, hours, group schedule, insurance list, fees, and license numbers were taken from the live site as of 2026-09-09. The clinic should confirm each is current.
  2. Supply photographs. Team portraits and, optionally, the building. Each labeled placeholder gets replaced and given a written alt text.
  3. Decide on BIPP and anger management fees. They are not published today. Publishing them would remove a phone call from the intake path for many visitors. Not publishing them is also a valid choice; the rebuild handles either.
  4. Point the domain. The rebuild is one file with no dependencies. It can be hosted anywhere and connected to thecapelligroup.com with a single DNS change at the current registrar.
