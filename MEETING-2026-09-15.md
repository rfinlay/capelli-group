## SAM'S OBJECTION: "How do they make money if it's zero cost? Is there a catch?"

He raised this three separate times and called it his single biggest concern:
"anytime I see free, then I'll see pop-up ads and all this."

He then read Netlify's pricing page aloud on the call and found two things Rick could not
answer live: **"private by default till you publish"** and the **"$300 credit limit a month."**

Rick promised to research and confirm. **ANSWER, verified at source 2026-09-15:**

### There are no ads. Ever.
Netlify has never run ads on hosted sites. Their revenue is paid plans (Personal $9/mo,
Pro $20/mo, Enterprise) and metered usage on those plans. Sam's specific fear is unfounded.

### "Private by default until you publish" does not mean the site stays private.
It describes deploy previews and unpublished builds, which are not publicly indexed until
published. A published production site is fully public. This is not a paywall.

### The "300 credits" is a monthly allowance, not a dollar credit.
Free plan: **300 credits/month, hard limit.** Credits are Netlify's internal usage unit.
Verified rates from Netlify's own pricing page:

| Resource | Cost |
|---|---|
| Production deploy | 15 credits each |
| Bandwidth | 20 credits per GB |
| Web requests | 2 credits per 10,000 requests |

### What this means for a therapy practice site — the arithmetic
A static brochure site of this size, at clinic traffic levels, consumes almost nothing.
The dominant cost is deploys: 300 credits ÷ 15 = **20 production deploys per month** with
zero bandwidth, or roughly 10 deploys plus ~5 GB of traffic. 5 GB is on the order of tens
of thousands of page views for a site like his.

**He will not approach the cap during normal operation.** Post-launch he is not deploying
at all unless he changes something.

### The honest catch, and it is worth telling him
**If the cap is ever exceeded on the Free plan, the site pauses** and visitors see a
"Site not available" page. Verified, Netlify's own words: *"Once your monthly credit
allotment is used up ... all of your web projects are paused and visitors to your web
projects will find a `Site not available` page."*

**Free plan cannot buy overage credits.** The site stays paused until the next billing
cycle or the account upgrades to Personal at $9/mo.

**Mitigation already in the project plan:** put his site on a **dedicated Netlify account**,
not Rick's multi-site account. The pause is account-wide, so isolation contains the blast
radius. This was decided 2026-09-09 in HOSTING-RESEARCH.md line 154 and it is now the
answer to Sam's question, not just an internal note.

**Auto-recharge is off by default** and unavailable on Free, so there is no surprise bill.
The failure mode is downtime, not a charge. That is the accurate framing for him:
the risk of "free" here is not a hidden invoice, it is a paused site under a traffic
spike that his practice will never generate.

### Also correct a thing Rick said on the call
Rick speculated the FTC would prevent Netlify from raising prices later. That is not a real
protection and should not be repeated. The durable protection is different and stronger:
**the deliverable is plain HTML files Sam owns.** If Netlify ever changes terms, the same
files move to any host in an afternoon. No lock-in is the answer, not regulators.
